## Plural endings

There are five main plural patterns in German. Every noun belongs to one of them.

| Pattern | Ending | Example singular | Example plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| -e | add **-e** | **die Blume** | **die Blumen** |
| -e (with umlaut) | add **-e**, stem vowel changes | **der Stuhl** | **die Stühle** |
| -er | add **-er** | **das Kind** | **die Kinder** |
| -er (with umlaut) | add **-er**, stem vowel changes | **das Buch** | **die Bücher** |
| -n / -en | add **-n** or **-en** | **die Frau** | **die Frauen** |
| -s | add **-s** | **das Auto** | **die Autos** |
| no change | no ending added | **der Lehrer** | **die Lehrer** |

## When to use which pattern

### -e

Common with many feminine nouns and some masculine and neuter ones.

- **die Tat → die Taten**
- **der Hund → die Hunde**

Many one-syllable masculine and neuter nouns with **a, o, u** take an umlaut as well.

- **der Stuhl → die Stühle**
- **der Sohn → die Söhne**
- **das Dorf → die Dörfer**

### -er

Mostly neuter nouns, often one-syllable. Many also take an umlaut.

- **das Kind → die Kinder**
- **das Buch → die Bücher**
- **das Wort → die Wörter** (different meaning from **die Worte**, see below)

### -n / -en

Most feminine nouns ending in **-e** take **-n**. Feminine nouns ending in a consonant and many foreign words take **-en**.

- **die Blume → die Blumen**
- **die Frau → die Frauen**
- **die Universität → die Universitäten**

This is the most predictable group. Nearly all feminine nouns follow this pattern.

All nouns ending in **-in** double the **-n**.

- **die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen**

### -s

Used mainly with words borrowed from other languages and with nouns ending in a vowel other than **-e**.

- **das Auto → die Autos**
- **das Hotel → die Hotels**
- **der Salon → die Salons**

### No ending

Most masculine nouns ending in **-er, -el, -en** and many neuter nouns ending in **-chen, -lein** add no ending.

- **der Lehrer → die Lehrer**
- **der Computer → die Computer**
- **das Mädchen → die Mädchen**

Some of these (especially one-syllable masculine nouns with **a, o, u**) still take an umlaut.

- **der Vater → die Väter**
- **der Bruder → die Brüder**
- **derApfel → die Äpfel**

## Nouns that only exist in the plural

Some German nouns have no singular form.

- **die Leute** (people)
- **die Eltern** (parents)
- **die Ferien** (holidays / vacation)
- **die Geschwister** (siblings)

## Plural in each case

Plural nouns decline across all four cases.

| Case | Plural article | Example (die Blumen) |
|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | **die** | **die Blumen** |
| Akkusativ | **die** | **die Blumen** |
| Dativ | **den** | **den Blumen** |
| Genitiv | **der** | **der Blumen** |

Note: in the dative plural, nouns also gain an **-n** unless the plural form already ends in **-n** or **-s**.

- **mit den Kinder-n** → **mit den Frauen** (no extra -n)

## Wörter vs. Worte

**die Wörter** refers to individual words (as in a vocabulary list), while **die Worte** refers to connected speech or someone's words.

- **Das sind schwierige Wörter.** = These are difficult words.
- **Seine Worte waren sehr bewegend.** = His words were very moving.

## Tips

- Always learn each noun with its plural form: **das Buch, die Bücher**.
- When in doubt, feminine nouns most often take **-n / -en**, neuter nouns often take **-er** or no change, and masculine nouns often take **-e** or no change.
- Foreign words and words ending in a vowel often take **-s**.
